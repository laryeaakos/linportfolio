01 / The Problem

Family organisation wasn’t failing because families lacked calendars.

Plans were scattered across calendars, messages, and memory. When life changed, responsibilities became easy to miss.

The challenge wasn’t simply scheduling more efficiently. It was reducing the mental effort of keeping family life together.
01

Mental overload

Too much responsibility lived in one person’s head.

02

Plan changes quickly

Routines shifted quickly, and updates were easy to miss.

03

Everything overlaps

School, home, work, and appointments competed for attention.

04

Home and work collide

Parents needed calm information at the right moment.

02 / Research

The research challenged my first assumption.

I started by thinking families needed one better calendar. The interviews pointed to a different problem.

Initial assumption

Families need one calendar that combines everything.

A cleaner calendar seemed like the obvious solution.

→
Research insight

Mental load and unexpected changes caused more stress.

The deeper problem was knowing what mattered now.

Pivot moment: Family overload is not always caused by missing tools. It often comes from too much scattered responsibility.
03 / System & Audience

A family system has more than one user.

The product is not only for one parent managing tasks. It supports multiple people who need different levels of visibility, responsibility, and access.

Research note
N

Neelam

Represents a parent balancing work, school, home tasks, and unexpected changes throughout the day.

P

Parents & guardians

Need a clear way to coordinate routines, reminders, and changes.

C

Children & family members

Need simple, visible guidance about what is happening next.

D

Different access needs

Need readable, calm, and accessible ways to understand information.

04 / From Insight to Concept

The solution didn’t need to become a better calendar.

I moved away from a “busy family calendar” idea toward a shared display focused on visibility, clarity, and the next useful action.

Calendar idea

One calendar for everything

Useful, but still required users to search, remember, and interpret too much.

→
New direction

A shared family noticeboard

Always visible at home, calm, and focused on what matters now.

05 / Exploring the Concept

Exploring how family information should appear at home.

I explored different ways of balancing shared visibility with a calm, non-overwhelming interface.

Direction 1

Everything in one view

Useful for overview, but can become visually heavy.

Direction 2

Shared noticeboard

Balanced visibility with calmer prioritisation.

Direction 3

One task at a time

Focused and calm, but risked hiding useful context.

Design realisation: Tasky needed to show what matters now, while still keeping the wider family rhythm visible.
06 / Product Strategy

Turning the concept into a focused product.

I defined a small set of priorities to keep Tasky focused and avoid turning the interface into another overwhelming planning tool.

Key product move

Make everyday family coordination easier to see, understand and change.

Priority 1

What matters now

Give family members a clear view of the next important thing.

Priority 2

Everyday coordination

Support routines, reminders, school events, and family tasks.

Priority 3

Is Tasky helping?

Keep the product focused on reducing effort, not adding more noise.

07 / Core User Flow

Designing around everyday moments.

Instead of opening many possible features, I focused on three moments that represent the core Tasky experience.

01 / Home

What matters right now?

A calm overview of the next important family moment.

02 / Ask

“What’s happening tomorrow?”

A simple way to ask for upcoming family plans.

03 / Change

Plans changed. Now what?

Help families understand changes without confusion.

Each screen answers the user’s immediate question while reducing the need to search, remember, or compare information.
08 / Voice Interaction

Making voice feel like part of the home.

Voice offers a familiar way to access family information without needing to stop, look down, or touch the screen.

09 / Final Solution

A shared family noticeboard built around what matters now.

Tasky brings schedules, responsibilities, and changing plans into one calm home experience without requiring families to constantly check another app.

10 / Reflection

Designing for coordination meant designing for attention.

The project shifted my thinking from “adding more information” to reducing effort and helping families understand what matters at the right time.

What changed

Calendar → shared family system

The research moved the concept beyond a simple scheduling tool.

Accessibility

More than one path

Voice, visibility, and calm hierarchy reduce effort for different users.

Next

Test in real homes

The next step would be testing readability, placement, and interaction flow in everyday routines.

Tasky taught me that reducing cognitive load is not about removing information — it is about giving information the right priority, at the right moment.
