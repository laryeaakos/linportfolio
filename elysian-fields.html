The Problem

Upload attendance-book.jpg

Relying on a manual, paper-based attendance book forced teachers to record entry times from memory later in the day. This delayed, recall-based workflow created high data inaccuracy, forgotten entries, and administrative friction during busy morning routines.

!
Core issue

Data accuracy was affected by delayed logging and reliance on human memory.

Research Summary

Conducted qualitative research using structured interviews with 5 key participants to map workflows and identify friction points.

✓

Initial Hypothesis

Assumed users wanted a complete workflow overhaul because of dissatisfaction with the attendance system.

×

The Reality

Users were satisfied with the familiar routine. The real failure was data accuracy caused by memory-based logging and delayed entries.

★

Key Insight

The solution wasn’t a better digital logbook — it was moving the touchpoint into the natural entry path.

Persona: Vivian

Vivian represents a busy early childhood educator whose morning attention is already focused on pupils, transitions, and classroom preparation.

Vivian

Early Child Educator

“Sometimes you forget the exact time you came in.”

Goals

  • Check in quickly without interrupting pupil supervision
  • Avoid relying on memory later in the day
  • Trust that attendance data is accurate

Frustrations

  • Busy mornings make sign-in easy to forget
  • Manual logs depend on delayed memory recall
  • Corrections create extra administrative strain

Journey Map Insight

Mapping Vivian’s arrival journey revealed that the attendance problem was not simply a form issue. The real opportunity was to move the check-in touchpoint into her natural physical path at the front door.

Arrival

Vivian enters the building already focused on pupils, bags, and classroom preparation.

Distraction

The paper book is not positioned as a natural, unavoidable step in her arrival flow.

Memory Gap

She may remember mid-day or later, then estimate her arrival time from memory.

Design Opportunity

Place an RFID kiosk along the entry path to make check-in immediate and effortless.

Design Evolution

Research and usability testing shifted the solution from a mobile attendance app to a more accessible physical kiosk experience.

Before Usability Studies

Mobile-based attendance idea

  • Required users to find and open their phones
  • Increased steps during a busy morning
  • Small touch targets created UI friction
→

After usability study

RFID kiosk in the entry path

  • One-step physical proximity interaction
  • Large visual target with glowing anchor circle
  • Clear confirmation with reset countdown

What Changed?

Key Iteration Insight

Before

Focused on a digital attendance entry and mobile-style interaction.

After

Shifted toward a physical RFID kiosk with fewer steps, stronger visibility, and a clearer arrival-path interaction.

Usability testing

Testing showed that the strongest reduction in cognitive load came from reducing decision points and placing the interaction directly in the arrival path.

Design Focus: Explore multiple directions, validate with users, and evolve toward a solution that is effortless, accessible, and accurate.
