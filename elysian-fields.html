UX Research Case Study

Elysian Fields Attendance Kiosk

An automated attendance kiosk designed to reduce morning clock-in friction through clear hierarchy, accessible identification, and a more reliable arrival workflow for teachers and administrators.

Project Overview

Designing around the real workflow

The project took approximately eight months and was led through a research-first process focused on cognitive behaviour, participant interviews, workflow analysis, and translating findings into the final kiosk interface.

Role

UX Researcher & Interaction Designer

Responsibilities

Cognitive research, participant interviews, workflow analysis, interaction design and high-fidelity kiosk UI.

Duration

Approximately eight months from research through final concept.

Understanding the User

The research changed the problem I was solving

I conducted structured interviews with five participants. I initially expected frustration with the existing attendance system to be the main issue. The interviews revealed something different.

Initial hypothesis

Staff disliked the existing paper attendance system and wanted a completely different digital experience.

→
What the research revealed

Participants were comfortable with the familiar routine. The real problem was delayed logging and unreliable memory, which led to inaccurate or forgotten attendance entries.

The main pain points

Delayed, memory-based logging

Teachers sometimes recorded attendance after arriving, which required them to remember the exact arrival time later.

The attendance book was easy to miss

The paper book was not naturally positioned within the arrival path, creating a prospective-memory problem: users had to remember to perform an action at the right moment.

Persona & Journey

Designing around Vivian’s arrival routine

Vivian represents a busy early-childhood educator who needs attendance recording to happen naturally as part of entering the building rather than becoming another task to remember later.

Persona — Vivian

Current journey

Key insight: The opportunity was not simply to build a better digital logbook. The attendance touchpoint needed to sit directly within the natural arrival path so the environment itself could help trigger the behaviour.
Starting Design

The first concept started as a mobile attendance app

Early mobile concepts explored a simple digital attendance flow. The goal was to give staff a quick way to identify themselves, view their arrival time, and confirm attendance with minimal interaction. Usability testing later revealed that the mobile format introduced unnecessary cognitive and practical friction, which ultimately shifted the solution toward an RFID-enabled kiosk.

Early mobile concept — Clock-in state

The initial clock-in state focused on three pieces of information: user identity, arrival time, and one clear primary action. The intention was to simplify the paper attendance process into a quick digital confirmation.

Early mobile concept — Confirmation state

The confirmation state provided immediate feedback after clocking in. Vivian’s identity, timestamp, and success message reassured the user that attendance had been recorded before the interface returned to its default state.

Usability Study

Testing disproved the mobile-first direction

I ran an unmoderated Maze study with the same five participants and used think-aloud feedback to understand how the concept fitted into their real working environment.

The smartphone assumption failed

Not every participant used a smartphone during work. Opening a personal device, navigating to the product and logging in introduced extra steps into a task that needed to happen quickly during arrival.

Confirmation needed stronger feedback

The confirmation interaction was too subtle and depended on a small target. Users needed stronger hierarchy and clearer confirmation that the attendance action had successfully completed.

Design pivot: Instead of asking staff to bring the attendance system with them on their phone, the solution became a persistent kiosk at the point of entry, using RFID identification to reduce the number of actions required.
Refining the Design

Making the kiosk easier to notice, understand and trust

The final design concentrated on clear identification, immediate confirmation and predictable reset behaviour.

Making the RFID interaction visible

Illuminated circular cues were introduced around the RFID interaction point to create a strong pre-attentive visual anchor and make the expected action easier to understand without reading extensive instructions.

Strengthening confirmation and closure

The success state uses a prominent confirmation symbol, the user’s name, arrival time and a visible reset indicator. The short reset period provides closure and makes the kiosk’s next action predictable.

Final interface states

The final experience supports the primary RFID journey while maintaining a manual identification route as a fallback when needed.

Accessibility

Accessibility shaped the interaction, not just the styling

The kiosk needed to remain understandable in a busy physical environment and accommodate different levels of motor precision, attention and visual ability.

1

Clear hierarchy and legibility

Larger text, strong contrast and simplified information hierarchy support reading while standing rather than requiring close inspection.

2

Reduced motor precision

RFID proximity removes the need to accurately target a small touchscreen control for the primary attendance action.

3

Predictable feedback

Immediate confirmation and a visible reset state communicate what happened and when the kiosk is ready for the next person.

Takeaway

Designing beyond the screen

“True UX maturity means designing for the user’s environment, not just the screen.”

Next Steps

Taking the concept into its real environment

Hardware prototype

Test the kiosk as a physical prototype in a real school entrance or hallway rather than only on a screen.

Multi-user stress testing

Observe how the interface performs when several staff members arrive within a short period.

Administrative integration

Explore integration with administrative dashboards, attendance records and payroll workflows.
