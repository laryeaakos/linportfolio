UX Case Study

Elysian Fields Attendance Kiosk

A research-led RFID kiosk concept designed to reduce morning attendance friction for teachers and staff through a clearer, faster, and more accessible check-in experience.

Role UX Researcher & Designer
Duration 8 months
Methods Interviews, Testing
Focus Accessibility
Teachers and staff relied on a paper attendance book during busy morning routines. Because entries were often completed later from memory, attendance records could become delayed, incomplete, or inaccurate.

What was happening?

The paper book did not naturally fit into the arrival flow. Teachers were focused on children, morning preparation, and immediate responsibilities, making check-in easy to forget.

Core issue

The problem was not simply the paper format. The real issue was that the attendance task depended on memory during a hectic moment.

Delayed recall led to forgotten check-ins and unreliable data.
Research Insight

The solution had to fit the real environment

Research showed that the best solution was not just a better digital logbook. The interaction needed to move into the teacher’s natural entry path, reducing steps and making check-in almost effortless.

01

Understand routines

Explore how teachers actually arrive, prepare, and move through the school environment.

02

Reduce steps

Remove unnecessary actions such as finding a phone, opening an app, or remembering to log later.

03

Design for clarity

Use a visible RFID kiosk, clear confirmation, and a simple fallback option for manual sign-in.

Design Evolution

From mobile attendance idea to physical RFID kiosk

Before usability testing

  • Users would need to find and open their phones.
  • The process increased steps during a busy morning.
  • Small touch targets created extra UI friction.

After usability testing

  • One-step physical proximity interaction.
  • Large visual target with a clear RFID anchor.
  • Confirmation screen with reset countdown for the next user.
Final Experience

A clearer check-in flow for staff

01

Tap ID card

The staff member holds their ID card near the RFID reader.

02

See confirmation

The kiosk gives visible feedback that attendance has been recorded.

03

Auto reset

The screen resets after a short countdown so the next staff member can check in.

Key takeaway

True UX maturity means designing for the user’s environment, not just the screen. In this project, the strongest design decision was reducing effort by placing the interaction where the user naturally arrives.
