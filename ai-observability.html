Interactive UX Case Study

AI Observability Investigation Assistant

A guided investigation experience that helps teams move from anomaly to understanding without confronting an entire monitoring system at once.

Interaction Design Dashboard UX Progressive Disclosure AI Monitoring
Design Challenge

Showing everything can still leave users unsure what to do.

Observability tools contain large amounts of valuable information, but during an incident the user’s problem is not simply access to data. They need to understand what changed, whether it matters, and where to investigate next.

Information overload

Dense dashboards force users to interpret several metrics and competing signals at once.

Unclear investigation path

Knowing that something changed does not automatically tell the user what deserves attention first.

Data without meaning

Monitoring information becomes more useful when it supports a decision rather than simply reporting a number.

Design Strategy

Turn investigation into a guided sequence.

Instead of exposing the entire observability environment immediately, the experience reveals information according to the decision the user needs to make at that moment.

01

Progressive disclosure

Reveal complexity gradually so each screen answers one clear investigation question.

02

Investigate before explaining

Let users gather evidence before presenting a conclusion about the likely root cause.

03

Actionable next steps

Every state should help the user decide where to look or what to do next.

Interactive Investigation

Follow the anomaly from signal to root cause

This prototype demonstrates the interaction strategy rather than a production monitoring environment. Move through the investigation one decision at a time.

Why This Interaction Works

The investigation is organised around three questions.

Question 01

What happened?

Start with the observable change and enough context to understand why it deserves attention.

Question 02

What does it mean?

Connect the anomaly to impact, recent changes and environmental evidence before presenting a conclusion.

Question 03

What should I do next?

End with a clear direction so the experience supports action, not just interpretation.

Accessibility

Reducing cognitive load without removing control.

Accessibility in this interaction is closely tied to predictability, information hierarchy, navigation and the amount of information the user is expected to process at once.

1

Clear hierarchy

Each state exposes one main question and one primary next action, reducing competition for attention.

2

More than colour

Status and severity combine labels, text and structural position rather than depending on colour alone.

3

Reversible navigation

Back, replay and exit controls give users predictable ways to revisit information or leave the experience without becoming trapped.

Reflection

Observability is also an information-sequencing problem.

What changed

I moved away from treating the dashboard as the primary experience. The investigation itself became the product flow, with the dashboard appearing only when its context became useful.

What I learned

Complex systems do not always need simpler data. Sometimes they need better sequencing — showing the right information when the user is ready to use it.
